Protecting the last white latitude

Northlight Refuge is a public sanctuary and research outpost dedicated to polar bears, sea ice, and the communities that share this shifting coast.

Why we exist

A living corridor between ice and open water

We track denning sites, map thinning ice, and host visiting scientists. Every program on this page is designed so a traveler, a donor, or a first-year researcher can find a way in.

  • 412 bears tagged since 2004
  • 19 active den surveys
  • 6 partner universities

From the field

Three seasons on the ice

Late winter patrol

Field teams log tracks, breathing holes, and the first signs of den emergence along the pack ice.

Spring traverse

When the light returns, we walk ridgelines that funnel bears toward remaining hunting grounds.

Polar night watch

Dark-season cameras and quiet snowmobile routes keep disturbance low while data still flows.

What we run

Three programs, one coastline

Bear watch

Non-invasive telemetry and community sighting logs help us keep a current map of mothers and cubs.

Ice ledger

Weekly thickness samples feed an open dataset used by mariners, hunters, and climate labs.

Shore school

Local students join short field modules on safety, identification, and respectful observation.

Fixed on the far ice

The refuge is not a fence. It is a promise to keep walking the same shore after the maps have changed.

Visitor briefing

Tours leave twice a week in late winter and once a week after breakup. Groups stay at eight people. Engines idle downwind. If a bear changes course toward the party, we abort and return to the hut — no photographs are worth a second encounter.

Bookings open on the first Monday of each month. Bring a quiet voice and leave drones at the station.

Field kit

Insulated boots rated to −40, a wind shell, glacier glasses, and a dry bag for cameras. The refuge issues a radio and a bear flare. Food stays sealed. Scented lotions stay in town.
